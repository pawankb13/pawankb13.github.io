# Prospect Theory and Loss Aversion

Behavioral Economics · Class 3

---

## A choice

**Option A:** a sure gain of ₹500

**Option B:** a 50% chance of ₹1,000, otherwise nothing

Which do you choose?

Note:
Take a show of hands. Most choose A. Record the split on the board.

---

## Now this choice

**Option C:** a sure loss of ₹500

**Option D:** a 50% chance of losing ₹1,000, otherwise nothing

Which do you choose?

Note:
Most now choose D. Same expected values, opposite risk attitudes.

---

## Three features of the value function

1. **Reference dependence:** outcomes are gains or losses from a reference point
2. **Loss aversion:** losses hurt more than equal gains please
3. **Diminishing sensitivity:** the first ₹100 matters more than the next

---

## The value function

$$
v(x) =
\begin{cases}
x^{\alpha} & x \ge 0 \\
-\lambda(-x)^{\beta} & x < 0
\end{cases}
$$

Tversky and Kahneman (1992) estimate $\alpha = \beta = 0.88$ and $\lambda = 2.25$.

---

## What this means for managers

- Frame price changes carefully: a surcharge feels worse than a lost discount
- Free trials create a reference point that makes cancelling feel like a loss
- Sales teams hold losing positions too long
