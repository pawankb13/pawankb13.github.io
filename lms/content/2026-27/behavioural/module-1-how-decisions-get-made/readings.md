### Start here

- [Simon, H. A. (1955). A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice. Quarterly Journal of Economics, 69(1), 99–118](https://doi.org/10.2307/1884852). The paper that started bounded rationality. Read sections I and II.
- [Kahneman, D. (2003). Maps of Bounded Rationality: Psychology for Behavioral Economics. American Economic Review, 93(5), 1449–1475](https://doi.org/10.1257/000282803322655392). Kahneman's Nobel lecture. A clear account of System 1, System 2 and why errors are systematic.

### Bounded rationality and satisficing

- [Simon, H. A. (1956). Rational Choice and the Structure of the Environment. Psychological Review, 63(2), 129–138](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0042769). Where the word "satisficing" first appears.
- [Conlisk, J. (1996). Why Bounded Rationality? Journal of Economic Literature, 34(2), 669–700](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2729218). Why economists should model limits on thinking, and the evidence for them.
- [Artinger, F. M., Gigerenzer, G. and Jacobs, P. (2022). Satisficing: Integrating Two Traditions. Journal of Economic Literature, 60(2), 598–635](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.20201396). A recent survey of satisficing in economics and psychology.
- [Schwartz, B., Ward, A., Monterosso, J., Lyubomirsky, S., White, K. and Lehman, D. R. (2002). Maximizing versus Satisficing: Happiness Is a Matter of Choice. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 83(5), 1178–1197](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1178). Maximisers report more regret and less satisfaction.

### Heuristics: error or adaptation?

- [Gigerenzer, G. and Gaissmaier, W. (2011). Heuristic Decision Making. Annual Review of Psychology, 62, 451–482](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-120709-145346). The case that simple rules often work well. Useful for the "failure or adaptation" question.

### Two systems and the three puzzles

- [Frederick, S. (2005). Cognitive Reflection and Decision Making. Journal of Economic Perspectives, 19(4), 25–42](https://doi.org/10.1257/089533005775196732). The source of the bat-and-ball puzzle.
- [Melnikoff, D. E. and Bargh, J. A. (2018). The Mythical Number Two. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 22(4), 280–293](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2018.02.001). The main critique of the two-systems view. Read it with Kahneman (2003).

### For managers

- [Kahneman, D., Lovallo, D. and Sibony, O. (2011). Before You Make That Big Decision. Harvard Business Review, 89(6), 50–60](https://hbr.org/2011/06/the-big-idea-before-you-make-that-big-decision). A 12-question checklist for spotting bias in a team's recommendation.
- [Beshears, J. and Gino, F. (2015). Leaders as Decision Architects. Harvard Business Review, 93(5), 52–62](https://hbr.org/2015/05/leaders-as-decision-architects). How managers can redesign the setting in which decisions are made. A preview of the prescriptive half of the course.

### Should, does, and what to do

- [Madrian, B. C. and Shea, D. F. (2001). The Power of Suggestion: Inertia in 401(k) Participation and Savings Behavior. Quarterly Journal of Economics, 116(4), 1149–1187](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355301753265543). The automatic enrolment example from Concept 5.

### Policy and India

- [Government of India (2019). Economic Survey 2018-19, Volume 1, Chapter 2: Policy for Homo Sapiens, Not Homo Economicus](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2019-20/economicsurvey/doc/vol1chapter/echap02_vol1.pdf). Behavioural economics applied to Swachh Bharat, Beti Bachao Beti Padhao and other schemes.
- [World Bank (2015). World Development Report 2015: Mind, Society, and Behavior](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/20597). How limited attention and mental models shape decisions in developing economies. Read the Overview.

### Overview of the field

- [Thaler, R. H. (2016). Behavioral Economics: Past, Present, and Future. American Economic Review, 106(7), 1577–1600](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.106.7.1577). A short history of the field and where it is going.

### Books

- Kahneman, D. (2011). Thinking, Fast and Slow. Farrar, Straus and Giroux. Parts I and II for this module.
- Thaler, R. H. (2015). Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics. W. W. Norton.
- Mullainathan, S. and Shafir, E. (2013). Scarcity: Why Having Too Little Means So Much. Times Books. On attention as a scarce resource.
- Bell, D. E., Raiffa, H. and Tversky, A. (Eds.) (1988). Decision Making: Descriptive, Normative, and Prescriptive Interactions. Cambridge University Press. The source of the should, does and what-to-do distinction.
