### Start here

- Thaler, R. H. (2015). Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics. W. W. Norton. Read the chapters on mental accounting and on "the beer on the beach".
- Ariely, D. (2008). Predictably Irrational. HarperCollins. Chapter 1, "The Truth about Relativity", covers the decoy effect and The Economist.
- [Anderson, E. T. and Simester, D. I. (2003). Mind Your Pricing Cues. Harvard Business Review, 81(9), 96–103](https://hbr.org/2003/09/mind-your-pricing-cues). Short and practical: sale signs, prices ending in 9 and price-match promises.

### Mental accounting and transaction utility

- [Thaler, R. H. (1985). Mental Accounting and Consumer Choice. Marketing Science, 4(3), 199–214](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.4.3.199). The source of transaction utility and the beer example.
- [Thaler, R. H. (1999). Mental Accounting Matters. Journal of Behavioral Decision Making, 12(3), 183–206](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(199909)12:3<183::AID-BDM318>3.0.CO;2-F). The best single review of the topic.
- [Kahneman, D. and Tversky, A. (1984). Choices, Values, and Frames. American Psychologist, 39(4), 341–350](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.39.4.341). The lost ticket and the lost note.
- [Hastings, J. S. and Shapiro, J. M. (2013). Fungibility and Consumer Choice: Evidence from Commodity Price Shocks. Quarterly Journal of Economics, 128(4), 1449–1498](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjt018). Mental accounting in real petrol purchases.
- [Epley, N., Mak, D. and Idson, L. C. (2006). Bonus or Rebate? The Impact of Income Framing on Spending and Saving. Journal of Behavioral Decision Making, 19(3), 213–227](https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.519). The same $25 is spent more when it is called a bonus.
- [Shah, A. K., Shafir, E. and Mullainathan, S. (2015). Scarcity Frames Value. Psychological Science, 26(4), 402–412](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614563958). A replication of the beer study, by income group.

### Reference prices and partitioned pricing

- [Anderson, E. T. and Simester, D. I. (2001). Are Sale Signs Less Effective When More Products Have Them? Marketing Science, 20(2), 121–142](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.20.2.121.10194). Field evidence on sale signs.
- [Morwitz, V. G., Greenleaf, E. A. and Johnson, E. J. (1998). Divide and Prosper: Consumers' Reactions to Partitioned Prices. Journal of Marketing Research, 35(4), 453–463](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224379803500404). The first study of partitioned prices.
- [Anderson, E. T. and Simester, D. I. (2003). Effects of $9 Price Endings on Retail Sales: Evidence from Field Experiments. Quantitative Marketing and Economics, 1(1), 93–110](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1023581927405). Three field experiments on prices ending in 9.
- [Chetty, R., Looney, A. and Kroft, K. (2009). Salience and Taxation: Theory and Evidence. American Economic Review, 99(4), 1145–1177](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.99.4.1145). Showing the tax on the shelf tag cut demand by about 8%.
- [Blake, T., Moshary, S., Sweeney, K. and Tadelis, S. (2021). Price Salience and Product Choice. Marketing Science, 40(4), 619–636](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1261). The StubHub experiment on fees shown late.
- Central Consumer Protection Authority (2023). Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023. Read the definitions of drip pricing, basket sneaking and subscription trap.

### Payment decoupling

- [Prelec, D. and Loewenstein, G. (1998). The Red and the Black: Mental Accounting of Savings and Debt. Marketing Science, 17(1), 4–28](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.17.1.4). The pain of paying.
- [Prelec, D. and Simester, D. (2001). Always Leave Home Without It: A Further Investigation of the Credit-Card Effect on Willingness to Pay. Marketing Letters, 12(1), 5–12](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008196717017). Cash against card for Celtics tickets.
- [Gourville, J. T. and Soman, D. (1998). Payment Depreciation: The Behavioral Effects of Temporally Separating Payments from Consumption. Journal of Consumer Research, 25(2), 160–174](https://doi.org/10.1086/209533). The health club billing study.
- [Soman, D. and Gourville, J. T. (2001). Transaction Decoupling: How Price Bundling Affects the Decision to Consume. Journal of Marketing Research, 38(1), 30–44](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=8904). The theatre bundle and the ski pass.

### Decoy, compromise and assortment

- [Huber, J., Payne, J. W. and Puto, C. (1982). Adding Asymmetrically Dominated Alternatives: Violations of Regularity and the Similarity Hypothesis. Journal of Consumer Research, 9(1), 90–98](https://doi.org/10.1086/208899). The original decoy study.
- [Simonson, I. and Tversky, A. (1992). Choice in Context: Tradeoff Contrast and Extremeness Aversion. Journal of Marketing Research, 29(3), 281–295](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224379202900301). The compromise effect.
- [Frederick, S., Lee, L. and Baskin, E. (2014). The Limits of Attraction. Journal of Marketing Research, 51(4), 487–507](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.12.0061). A careful test of when the decoy effect fails.
- [Wu, C. and Cosguner, K. (2020). Profiting from the Decoy Effect: A Case Study of an Online Diamond Retailer. Marketing Science](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1231). The decoy effect in real sales data.
- [Sharpe, K. M., Staelin, R. and Huber, J. (2008). Using Extremeness Aversion to Fight Obesity. Journal of Consumer Research, 35(3), 406–422](https://doi.org/10.1086/587631). The compromise effect in soft drink sizes.
- [Iyengar, S. S. and Lepper, M. R. (2000). When Choice is Demotivating: Can One Desire Too Much of a Good Thing? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 79(6), 995–1006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995). The jam study.
- [Scheibehenne, B., Greifeneder, R. and Todd, P. M. (2010). Can There Ever Be Too Many Options? A Meta-Analytic Review of Choice Overload. Journal of Consumer Research, 37(3), 409–425](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235). Read this alongside the jam study.
- Iyengar, S. S., Huberman, G. and Jiang, W. (2004). How Much Choice is Too Much? Contributions to 401(k) Retirement Plans. In O. S. Mitchell and S. P. Utkus (eds), Pension Design and Structure, Oxford University Press, 83–96. Choice overload in retirement savings.
